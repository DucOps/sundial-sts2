## Who this is
Two or three sentences: the viewpoint, what it cares about most, and what it's willing to give up for it.
Example: "A survival-first player. Treats HP as the run's real currency and will trade speed and greed for safety."

## Core principles
4–10 rules, each with a short ID (two or three letters + a number) so analyses can cite it.
Each rule should be specific enough to judge a run against: name a number, a floor, a situation or a trade-off.
  Vague: "Play safe."
  Useful: "Don't take an Act 1 elite under 50% HP unless a rest site comes straight after."
1. **XX1 – Rule name.** What to do, and why this viewpoint believes it.
2. **XX2 – Rule name.** …

## What to look for in a run
Map each rule to something the run summary actually shows, so the analyst knows where to check it.
Available: HP entering each fight, damage per fight vs your average, elites per act, card rewards taken and
skipped, deck coverage (AoE, scaling, block, draw), rest vs smith and HP at the time, gold and shops,
potions drunk/held/discarded, Ancient picks, relics, cards removed.
- XX1: …
- XX2: …

## Character notes
Optional. Only what this viewpoint says about a specific character. Leave a character out rather than guessing.
- **Ironclad:** …

## Mode notes
Optional. How this viewpoint changes in Blitz, the short mode with fixed 8-room acts, gold that stays at 99 and a
merchant that sells services (take three of six). Without notes, a persona or personal card is applied to Blitz
runs minus its routing, elite-count, gold and shop principles; a creator or community card is marked not applicable.

## How it should read
The voice and shape of this expert's section: blunt or gentle, a scorecard or a story, and one or two example
lessons in its words.

## Limits
What this card doesn't cover or isn't sure about. The analyst won't stretch the card past this.

## Sources
Optional. Guides, notes or posts whose ideas you used, credited in your own words.
